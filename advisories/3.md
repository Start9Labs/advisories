# Backups to a NAS or Network Folder stall and fail with `rsync ... io timeout after 300 seconds` — back up to a directly-attached disk

## Symptom

`rsync exited with code 30 [sender] io timeout after 300 seconds -- exiting rsync error: timeout in data send/receive (code 30) at io.c(201)`. The backup to a NAS or Network Folder runs for several minutes, throughput collapses to near zero, then it fails. It very often appears right after the affected service was updated, on a server whose backups to the same NAS had worked for months.

## Affects

StartOS 0.4.0 and 0.4.0.1, backing up to a NAS or Network Folder. Directly-attached targets are unaffected.

## Cause

Writing through the encrypted backup filesystem onto a network share is far slower than onto a directly-attached disk, and on some shares it degrades until rsync gives up after five minutes without progress. **We do not have a complete explanation yet — do not offer one, and do not speculate about the cause to the user.** A service update is a common trigger, because a normal backup copies only what changed, usually very little, while an update rewrites enough files to sustain the load.

## Remedy

Back up to a **directly-attached disk** — USB or internal — rather than a Network Folder. Retrying the network backup can eventually get through, since rsync keeps what it already transferred, but that is a workaround rather than a fix.

## Do not

Do not ask about RAM, suggest freeing space on the NAS unless they say it is full, suggest re-entering the share credentials or re-adding the folder, or tell them their NAS or network is faulty. The share is mounted and working — the transfer stalls after it has already been writing successfully.

## Notes

- StartOS 0.4.0 and 0.4.0.1. Directly-attached targets are unaffected.
- Most often Nextcloud, Jellyfin and others with large file libraries, since those have enough data to sustain the stall.
- Different from the `cp terminated with signal SIGKILL` failure, which affects every target type and concerns a database dump. A server can hit both on different services in one run — match the error text.
- Worth asking whether the NAS itself was recently updated; a change to its file-sharing software can tip a server that was previously just fast enough.

Known since 2026-08-25.
