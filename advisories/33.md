# Jellyfin 12.0: HDR transcodes on AMD VA-API/Vulkan look too dark — set Tone mapping range to "pc" until 12.1

## Symptom

HDR (HDR10 or Dolby Vision) content played back with transcoding looks much darker than before, especially in dark scenes, after upgrading to Jellyfin 12.0. Inspecting the generated FFmpeg command shows the `libplacebo` filter running with no explicit range flag, and forcing a transcode reproduces the problem consistently on the same file that looked correct before.

## Affects

Jellyfin 12.0 installs that transcode HDR→SDR using an AMD GPU with VA-API + Vulkan interop (the `libplacebo` tone-mapping path). Not related to OpenCL-based tone mapping or non-AMD GPUs.

## Cause

`libplacebo` does not support limited-range RGB output but still allows frames to be flagged as limited range while the underlying pixel values stay full-range. Mesa 25 (used by the jellyfin-ffmpeg 7 build in 12.0's predecessor) treated RGB as full-range regardless, masking the mismatch. Mesa 26 (bundled with jellyfin-ffmpeg 8 in 12.0) respects the limited-range flag, so the subsequent VAAPI RGB-to-YUV conversion incorrectly expands the range, crushing blacks and blowing out whites.

## Remedy

Fixed by 12.1. Until upgrading, set the Tonemapping Range option to "pc" in the transcoding settings — this makes the range flag match the actual full-range data and avoids the incorrect expansion, restoring normal-looking output.

## Do not

- Do not change TonemappingPeak — it is working as intended; it overrides the HDR *source* brightness, not the SDR target, and is unrelated to this darkness issue.
- Do not enable peak detection to work around this — it is very slow on integrated GPUs and does not address the actual cause.

## Notes

The FFmpeg command itself is unchanged from earlier versions; the regression comes from the Mesa 26 VAAPI driver now honoring range flags that jellyfin-ffmpeg previously left mismatched. Reports of "video is dark since upgrading to 12.0" on AMD hardware with HDR content are almost always this, not a tone-mapping-algorithm or mastering-metadata problem.
